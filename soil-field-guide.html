Putting a Name to Your Soil

Walk through the walkover test, the hand test, and the pit test to work out what you're growing in — plus a quick read on drainage and pH from the weeds already there.

Choose a day when the soil is moist and dig a pit 2 ft × 2 ft × 2 ft. Answer what you find, then see your results below. This pit doubles as a drainage-test hole later on.

These signs read independently of each other — check off whatever applies to your plot.

Several kits test the nitrogen, phosphate, potash and pH content of the soil. Some use test tubes and indicator solutions; others use a probe you push straight into the ground.

These kits give a reasonably accurate read on whether your soil needs liming — but they're less reliable for pinning down exact fertilizer needs, since translating the readings into a practical feeding plan is the hard part.

Based on the classic four-test method — walkover, hand, pit and soil kit — for identifying soil type, drainage and pH without lab equipment.
